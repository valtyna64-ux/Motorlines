The Car That Taught Me Freedom

ML – Between high school, first adventures, and a family car that ended up defining an unforgettable chapter of life.

May 21, 2026 | 5:00 PM

"I got my first car in 2000. It wasn't new, but it was a really fun car. I learned so much with it, and I even drove it to high school," recalls an anonymous owner. He explains that, at 18 years old, he had to repeat his final year of high school. On the bright side, his driving lessons had already been paid for, and by December 2000 he had earned his driver's license. The following year, he used the car every day to drive to school. Although it wasn't the newest or most luxurious vehicle, it meant a great deal to him. It was the family's Peugeot 106. "It was a very simple car, but incredibly fun to drive and very well cared for. It had its little quirks—for example, it tended to stall when the engine was cold—but I learned a lot from it."

Why Did the Peugeot 106 Stall When Cold?

In carbureted engines, fuel does not vaporize as easily when the engine is cold. To make starting easier, these vehicles were equipped with a choke—either manual or automatic—that temporarily enriched the air-fuel mixture until the engine reached its normal operating temperature. If the choke was not used correctly or was disengaged too early, the engine could stall. Source: Engineer Fix, "How a Choke Works to Start a Cold Engine."

Peugeot 106 and Citroën Saxo

Although they belong to different brands, both vehicles were developed by the PSA Group. They share several structural components, mechanical parts, transmissions, and engines, while each maintained its own distinctive design and personality.

106 Électrique

The Peugeot 106 Électrique was introduced in 1995 and is considered one of the first mass-produced electric cars in Europe. It used nickel-cadmium (Ni-Cd) batteries, reached a top speed of approximately 90 km/h (56 mph), and offered a driving range of between 80 and 100 kilometers (50–62 miles), making it primarily designed for urban driving.

A European Success

The Peugeot 106 was produced between 1991 and 2003, with nearly 2.8 million units built, making it one of the French brand's most successful compact cars.

Source: Stellantis Media; Peugeot 106.

It Debuted as a Three-Door

When it was introduced in 1991, the Peugeot 106 was available only as a three-door hatchback. A five-door version was launched the following year to appeal to a wider audience.

Source: Stellantis Media.

A Small Champion

Thanks to its lightweight construction and excellent handling, the Peugeot 106 competed in numerous rally and motorsport events, especially across Europe, where it remains a favorite among driving enthusiasts.

Source: Peugeot 106; Motor1.

Shared Its Platform

The Peugeot 106 shared much of its platform and mechanical components with the Citroën Saxo, simplifying development and allowing many parts to be shared between both models.

Source: Peugeot 106.

A GTi Version Was Available

Equipped with a 1.6-liter 16-valve engine producing 120 horsepower, the Peugeot 106 GTi is widely regarded as one of the most enjoyable hot hatchbacks of its era thanks to its outstanding power-to-weight ratio.

Source: Peugeot 106; Exame.

Now Considered a Youngtimer

More than 30 years after its debut, the earliest Peugeot 106 models are now considered youngtimers—modern classics that have become increasingly desirable among collectors.

Care & Maintenance

To keep a Peugeot 106 in excellent condition, it is recommended to change the engine oil and filter according to the manufacturer's service schedule, regularly inspect the cooling system, monitor the condition of the timing belt, check the spark plugs and ignition system, and inspect the brakes, suspension, and tire pressure. On older vehicles, it is also advisable to examine hoses, belts, and check for oil or coolant leaks to prevent breakdowns and maintain long-term reliability.

Source: Peugeot 106 Workshop Manual.
